# Copywriting

Create useful copy for the requested audience, offer, channel, and action. Match the depth to the assignment: three headlines need three headlines, not a campaign workshop.

## Establish the brief and evidence

Use the user's brief, supplied copy, and available brand documents first. Preserve accepted positioning and voice. Identify the offer, audience awareness, desired action, channel constraints, and supported proof. Ask only for missing information that materially blocks the work; otherwise state a small assumption and draft.

Use search or page access when the task needs outside evidence and the host provides it. Follow the host's tool instructions and actual tool schemas. Do not invent search capabilities, claim to have opened inaccessible URLs, or require a particular provider. Without access, work from supplied material and identify the specific evidence still needed. If the task is to review an unavailable page, request its text or an accessible attachment rather than inventing findings.

**Evidence boundary:** Never invent testimonials, quotations, customer counts, credentials, studies, performance metrics, prices, offer terms, guarantees, urgency, or scarcity. A more precise number is not better unless supported. Remove unsupported claims or use clearly labeled placeholders outside publishable copy. Do not merely soften an unsupported factual promise with adjectives. A guarantee describes actual offer terms; it is not proof of results. Do not promise conversion lifts.

The reference library contains historical frameworks, example copy, numerical illustrations, and practitioner claims. Treat these as prompts for judgment and testing, not evidence about the user's business or universal performance guarantees. Verify any attribution or benchmark before presenting it as an established fact; otherwise omit it or label it unverified. Do not manufacture missing policy, licensing, privacy, or regulatory terms.

## Write

1. **Choose the opening from audience awareness.** For unaware/problem-aware readers, establish a relevant situation or problem. For solution-aware readers, explain the approach. For product-aware/most-aware readers, lead with the differentiator or offer. These are hypotheses, not fixed rules.
2. **Give the piece one main job.** Use AIDA (attention, interest, desire, action), PAS (problem, agitation, solution), or another relevant structure when useful. Address the reader's real situation without exaggerating fear or consequences.
3. **Connect features to supported benefits.** Use concrete language, relevant demonstrations, and verified proof. The SUCCESs lens—simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, stories—can strengthen the message without forcing every element into it. Reciprocity, social proof, authority, and scarcity apply only when truthful and relevant.
4. **Make the next step clear.** State the action, actual offer terms, and what happens next. Use deadlines and risk reversal only when supplied or verified. A valid offer need not have a deadline or a guarantee.
5. **Edit for the channel.** Check message continuity from headline to lead to CTA, requested length, skimmability, and voice. Offer meaningful variants when requested or useful; do not inflate the deliverable count.

For physical mail, consider recipient/list fit and offer before polishing sentences. Coordinate envelope, letter, PS, response device, and follow-up only within the requested package. Personal presentation must not imply a false relationship or disguise the sender's identity.

## Review

Read the supplied piece in context, then use the relevant Seven Sweeps:

- **Clarity:** Can the reader understand the offer and main idea?
- **Voice:** Does it preserve the brand and fit the reader's situation?
- **So what:** Does each feature connect to a relevant, supportable benefit?
- **Prove it:** What evidence supports each factual claim?
- **Specificity:** Can vague language become concrete without inventing details?
- **Emotion:** Does it reflect the reader's experience without manipulation or overstatement?
- **Risk and next step:** Are actual terms, hesitations, and the action explained honestly?

Fix the material issues and recheck the whole piece. Deliver revised copy and a short explanation of consequential changes. Scores, if requested, are editorial heuristics—not measurements of conversion potential or permission to launch. A quick review does not require seven visible reports.

## Deliver and check

Return the requested copy or review in the user's format. Write files only when useful and supported; otherwise provide the content directly. Identify unresolved factual placeholders and any limitation that affects usability. Do not claim a file was created, research performed, or a campaign sent unless it happened. Drafting copy does not itself authorize publication, ad spend, or sending messages.

For requested tests, specify the hypothesis, primary business metric, meaningful guardrails, and decision rule. Sample size and duration depend on baseline rate, detectable effect, uncertainty, and traffic patterns; fixed response counts in a reference are not universal validity thresholds. Separate test proposals from actual measured outcomes.
